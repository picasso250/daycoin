# The DayCoin Manifesto

*DayCoin · One Coin One Day · Discussion draft v0.2 · October 2026*

> **Join freely. Move toward fairness.**
>
> Freedom is the principle. Equality is the goal. Experimentation is the method.

This manifesto is a draft to be questioned, revised and challenged. It is neither investment advice nor a technical specification for a deployed contract. **DayCoin** is the project brand and long-term protocol family. Its first generation, **DC1**, is deployed on Ethereum Mainnet (Chain ID 1, contract `0x5473aac7ed5c5b14c4E7e2c8a8844f8e3FC92715`), with ERC-20 Name `DayCoin V1` and Symbol `DC1`. The previously deployed **UBIC1** is a separate historical experiment that cannot be renamed, revoked or upgraded. These names do not refer to one on-chain asset. Each contract's capabilities are determined by its code and verification evidence.

## 1. Money should not belong only to a few

We live in an age of growing productive capacity. Technology lets us produce more goods and services, but how wealth is distributed still depends on institutions we create. Money is among the most important of them.

**Who gets to create money? Who receives it when it is created?**

Bitcoin inspired us. It showed that people can voluntarily use a digital asset with no central issuer, governed by public rules. We respect that achievement. We also want to explore beyond it.

Decentralization does not automatically make distribution fair. Computing power, energy, capital, technical skill and time of entry still shape access to newly issued money. Newcomers can acquire bitcoin through exchange, but that is different from participating in its original issuance.

We want to try another arrangement: **keep opportunities to receive newly issued money open to newcomers, and narrow the differences in those opportunities between people.**

We have not proved any distribution perfectly fair, nor do we dismiss the risks early participants take. We simply believe that Bitcoin's creation did not exhaust the possibilities of monetary design.

## 2. Freedom is the principle. Equality is the goal.

We seek both freedom and equality without pretending they always fit together easily.

Freedom means choosing whether to join, hold, trade, leave or create competing rules, without asking a founder's permission.

Equality means making access to new issuance as independent as possible of how much capital a person owns, where they were born or when they entered the world.

**We will not manufacture the appearance of equality by building a new center of power that can arbitrarily scrutinize, exclude or control participants.**

Nor will we claim that people are equal merely because the same rules apply to every address.

When freedom and equality conflict, we protect the space for voluntary participation, exit and competition, and openly acknowledge the inequalities that remain. We choose to keep experimenting, rather than call a compromise a victory.

## 3. Scarcity need not be money's only ideal

Bitcoin chose a supply cap and periodic halvings. We want to study another path: **ongoing issuance, without a predetermined end to issuance.**

If the number of eligible addresses stays constant and each receives new coins at a fixed rate, the absolute amount added can stay constant while its proportion of existing supply falls over time.

If eligible participation grows, total issuance can grow with it. Such a response might soften some price shocks from adoption. It could also create new volatility through speculation, claims and selling.

We do not know the answer. Continuous issuance guarantees neither stable prices nor appreciation. More participating addresses do not mean a proportional rise in real population, consumption or production.

**Make supply rules transparent. Leave prices to the market. Put hypotheses to the test.**

We do not intend to rush into changing chosen issuance rules merely to make an inflation figure look smaller.

## 4. Money can have value as money

A digital asset need not have an industrial use for people to want to hold it.

People may value its verifiability, transferability, capacity to store value and possibility of future acceptance. Bitcoin's history encourages us to take that possibility seriously.

But a possibility is not a guarantee of value.

Some buy because they expect appreciation. Some participate in issuance because they expect output to exceed the opportunity cost of their ETH. Others may simply want money governed by different rules. All three motives can coexist. None guarantees the arrival of the next user.

DayCoin must compete on whether its rules deserve lasting voluntary adoption, not on a promise that its price will rise.

## 5. Public rules for issuance. Personal choice in burning.

New issuance should follow rules published in advance and open to verification, rather than a founder's response to price movements.

Holders should be free to dispose of tokens they legitimately own, including voluntary destruction where the specific contract supports it.

Our principle is therefore:

**Public rules for issuance. Personal choice in burning.**

A founder or any participant may voluntarily spend their own money to buy and burn tokens. That is not a protocol price guarantee or compulsory buyback. It grants no privilege to rewrite issuance rules.

Burning reduces supply but does not guarantee a higher market price, and any benefit need not be distributed equally. We will not present voluntary burning as a sure-profit mechanism.

## 6. A first step is not a fulfilled ideal

Our original ambition was simple: **One Person, One Coin, Every Day.**

But one natural person can control countless blockchain addresses. Without a trustworthy proof of uniqueness that does not abuse its power, one share per address is never one share per person.

The historical UBIC1 experiment used ETH deposits to establish claim eligibility. Eligible addresses could receive tokens under its rules, while people with more capital could manage more addresses and receive more shares.

**It was not universal basic income, and it did not achieve equality of wealth.**

DC1 is deployed as a separate contract and does not automatically change old UBIC1. Its Mainnet address and rules are public in the [README](README.md). A statement of ideals must not be mistaken for an on-chain promise.

We are willing to take the first step, and to record honestly how far it remains from the destination.

## 7. A founder should not hold permanent power

We do not want trust in a system to depend on a founder remaining benevolent forever.

Our direction is public source, fixed core rules and rejection of arbitrary upgrades and administrator privileges. Anyone should be able to inspect, question, leave or build another system.

A deployed, non-upgradeable contract does not automatically cease to exist because its developer changes a name or releases a successor. A future DC2, DC3 or differently named competitor gains no inherent legitimacy from a higher number or the same author.

New experiments must earn trust through their own design, evidence and voluntary adoption. Migration must not be forced; value is not automatically inherited.

**If the founder leaves one day, we hope to leave rules that can keep running independently, not followers waiting for instructions.**

## 8. We do not promise success

Nobody may use DayCoin.

It may fail to generate lasting monetary demand, or fail because of capital advantage, identity problems, contract defects, transaction costs or speculation. Its tokens may have no liquidity, and their price may fall to zero.

Open source is not a security audit. A contract that cannot be upgraded may also be difficult to repair. Depositing ETH is not a risk-free return, and tokens confer no redemption right over deposited principal.

We want to publish assumptions, mathematical models, implementation details and records of failure, so that others can test and even overturn our judgments.

Idealism does not mean refusing evidence. Quite the opposite: **if a system has real merit, it should withstand criticism.**

## 9. An invitation

We do not ask anyone to believe in DayCoin or buy its tokens.

We welcome critics who find code vulnerabilities, flawed economic models, unfair distribution or inconsistent values. We welcome anyone who borrows the idea and builds something better, without seeking our permission.

The question is not whether a founder can successfully launch a coin. It is this:

**Can an ordinary person, without coercive power, create a system that gradually makes distribution fairer through public rules, voluntary participation and persistence, without first conquering society?**

Bitcoin gave us reason to believe that another monetary system can be created.

We want to keep trying.

**Join freely. Move toward fairness.**
